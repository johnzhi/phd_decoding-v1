## Sections and scope

### S01 — p1

**scope:** Title, authors, affiliation, keywords and introduction

**assessment:** AISTech 2023 proceedings, printed pp404–412; Kawashiri, Kashihara and Fukada, JFE Steel. Motivation spans Japanese steel emissions, operational efficiency limits, hydrogen reduction and CCU market limitations. ### S02 — p1, 2

**scope:** Calculation procedure: Carbon Recycling Technology

**assessment:** CH4 is synthesised from recovered CO2 and external H2, then injected as a reducing agent. Figure 1 is conceptual. Methanation releases heat by the product-side convention in Eq1. The introduction's ideal claim that recycled carbon does not leave the system requires the stated recycle accounting; no measured plant closure or upstream utility inventory is supplied.

### S03 — p2, 3

**scope:** Calculation procedure: Rist Diagram

**assessment:** A heat/mass-balance operating line, equilibrium point and shaft efficiency estimate reducing-agent consumption. The paper explicitly excludes carbon in recycled methane AND injected preheating gas from specific carbon consumption. This boundary is essential to interpreting its percentage reductions.

### S04 — p3

**scope:** Constraints: theoretical flame temperature; top gas temperature

**assessment:** TFT is constrained to 2000–2300°C, assuming complete raceway conversion of C/H/N/O to CO/H2/N2. Coke temperature is taken as 0.75 TFT; its absolute-temperature convention is unresolved. Top gas is held at least 100°C by 1000°C preheating gas when necessary.

### S05 — p3, 4

**scope:** Constraints: thermal reserve zone and shaft efficiency

**assessment:** Conventional equilibrium-based Rist reasoning becomes inappropriate when a reserve zone disappears under high O2. The authors assume upper-shaft preheating creates a quasi-reserve zone, extrapolate its temperature linearly from 1000 to 800°C as blast O2 rises 35 to100%, and keep shaft efficiency constant. These are modelling assumptions informed by cited experiments, not demonstrated commercial performance.

### S06 — p4

**scope:** Constraints: direct reduction carbon

**assessment:** Eqs2–4 distinguish exothermic CO reduction, endothermic H2 reduction and strongly endothermic direct carbon reduction. Direct carbon cannot be negative; the Rist line must not enter the lower-right region relative to (1,0).

### S07 — p4, 5, 6

**scope:** Calculation Result

**assessment:** Common heat loss, shaft efficiency and outlet temperatures are fixed. CN-methane and hydrogen sweeps use different injection ranges with O2 enrichment. Figures4–5 show feasible TFT regions, carbon-reduction contours and preheating requirements. Table1 compares maximum-reduction cases against a separate conventional baseline; Fig6 illustrates one material flow.

### S08 — p6, 7

The heat-demand reduction is attributed to replacing endothermic direct reduction; methane oxidation also supplies heat. Figure8 is a comparative balance, not a capture-to-ship utility inventory.

### S09 — p7, 8

**scope:** Discussion: Change of Carbon Balance; Technologies to be Developed and Issues for Realization

**assessment:** Figure9 includes recycled methane carbon, explaining why total carbon entering tuyeres can increase while counted fossil carbon decreases. Kinetic modelling, laboratory/small-furnace validation, tuyere/body design, impurity-tolerant methanation and green H2 supply remain development requirements. The demonstration planned for2025 is a statement made in2023, not evidence that it occurred.

## Variables, units and assumptions

- BF: blast furnace; HM: tonne of hot metal; PC: pulverised coal; CN methane: methane whose recycled carbon is excluded from the counted fossil-carbon input. CN is an accounting label, not evidence of zero lifecycle emissions.

- TFT: theoretical flame temperature2000–2300°C; TGT: top-gas temperature with minimum100°C. Coke temperature0.75TFT has unresolved temperature-scale convention. Furnace outlet hot metal/slag1500°C; preheating gas1000°C; room-temperature oxygen/blast case25°C.

- TR: thermal reserve-zone temperature; assumed1000→800°C as O2 rises35→100%. The quasi-reserve zone and constant shaft efficiency are assumptions based on cited small-furnace work, not a kinetic derivation.

- X andY are gas/element-equivalent ratios as printed inFig2. Fe denotes iron basis; H2, CO, CO2 andH2O enter gas-equilibrium ratios. No full mole-to-mass implementation or equilibrium constants are supplied.

- RP: Rist operating line; P: lower-furnace heat-balance point; W: wüstite/iron equilibrium atTR; R: actual-operation point displaced fromW; G: upper reference point; GR/GW: shaft efficiency0.9. U andV are diagram labels without explicit independent prose definitions. Yd: apparent direct-reduction contribution; direct carbon minimum0kg/t-HM.

- Heat loss420MJ/t-HM is a common fixed input. Carbon-consumption values arekg-C/t-HM; coke/PC/H2/CH4 injection rows arekg/t-HM. Coke mass alone is not total counted carbon.

- Nm3/t-HM andNm3-dry/t-HM are normal-volume quantities. Normal reference temperature/pressure and wet/dry conversions are not fully specified; no unsupported mass conversion is used. Figure6 volumes are all per tonne hot metal.

- Blast O2 is volume concentration%; hot-stove O2 ceiling29% applies within the stove, while100% refers to the separate oxygen-blast case. Conventional baseline24% is distinct from the25–100% swept cases.

- Methane sweep0–200kg/t-HM with PC removed; H2 comparison0–60kg/t-HM. No uncertainty distribution or sensitivity range is given for shaft efficiency, heat loss, feed properties or green-H2 emissions.

- Figure4/5 direct-reduction contours80/40/0kg-C/t-HM and shaded TFT bounds define feasibility only within the chosen steady balance assumptions. Reaction kinetics, impurity processing, capture/methanation utilities, oxygen production, H2 generation and life-cycle assets are outside the demonstrated numerical inventory.

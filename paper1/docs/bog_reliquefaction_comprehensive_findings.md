## Process and physical assumptions

The six process/pressure cases compare conventional ammonia refrigeration, two-stage separation with ammonia, and two-stage separation with mixed ethane/propane/normal-butane/iso-butane refrigerants. Cases 1 use 6.5 bar and Cases 2 use 15 bar. Figure 3 shows compressor, exchanger, expansion, separator, purge and recycle connections; its two-stage cases return gas to different compression locations. A diagram and selected stream states do not specify the complete recycled-flow solution.

The study assumes ten 5000 m³ tanks, 98% laden filling, 5% ballast heel, mass-based BOR of 0.1%/day, 0.05–0.15 mol% nitrogen and BOG inlet temperatures of −40/−20°C. These are design scenarios, not observations of a Japanese route. Volume fill, vapor mole fraction, nitrogen fraction and mass BOR have different denominators. Equation 1 is a volume relation; its analytical inversion is `x = (1−f)/[(1−f)+f(vvap/vliq)]`. Independent examples verify the algebra without supplying an actual mixture flash.

The exact overall-feed versus liquid-phase nitrogen specification, equilibrium temperature, binary parameters and evolving tank composition are needed to reproduce Figure 2's filling-dependent enrichment. The selected nitrogen-only model does not establish behavior for all captured streams. The introduction's LNG efficiency and 12–13-day tank-holding examples retain their original case boundaries; they are not universal CO₂ operating rules. A sentence calling the 15-bar liquefaction temperature lower conflicts with the paper's higher-temperature explanation elsewhere.

Process constraints include 75% compressor adiabatic efficiency, maximum stage ratio 3, 30°C cooling water, 5 K cooler approach, 3 K HX5 approach, vapor compressor inlets and S8 temperature at least −55°C. The temperature bound does not independently validate the CO₂/N₂ solid boundary, every cold location or transient dry-ice safety. PSO bounds, seeds, penalties, convergence evidence and native property/case files are missing.

## Emissions accounting and percentage conflicts

Equations 2–6 count CO₂ purged from cargo plus CO₂ generated by MGO combustion for compression power. Laden and ballast operation each receive 4000 h/year. The fixed generator assumptions, 197.5 g fuel/kWh and 3.206 tCO₂/t fuel, imply **0.633185 kgCO₂/kWh**. The twelve Figure 6 power totals independently reproduce the corresponding Figure 5 combustion quantities within their displayed precision. This checks units and accounting; it does not independently predict compressor duty. Missing ballast power/purge partitions stay missing.

The displayed annual totals support the abstract's maximum reductions of **71.6024% at 6.5 bar** and **35.6297% at 15 bar**, relative to the corresponding conventional process. Those are partial-process reductions under fixed cases. They exclude propulsion, other auxiliaries, cooling pumps, upstream fuel, refrigerant leakage/assets and capture/injection lifecycle terms. They are not full-chain net avoided CO₂e or retained-cargo fractions.

## Appendix parameters that cannot be accepted as supplied

Table A1's laden Case 1-3 at 0.05 mol% nitrogen lists refrigerant fractions **26.89 + 57.62 + 5.74 + 14.75 = 105 mol%**. The row is preserved and excluded from runnable-case inputs. Renormalizing it would create a different case without identifying the source error. Other mixture totals are compatible with 100% at displayed precision.

Two pressure pairs also disagree. At 0.10 mol% nitrogen, Case 2-3 has A1 `R5/R6 = 2.24/16.73 bar`, while A4 gives `16.73/2.24 bar`. At 0.15 mol%, Case 1-3 has A1 `2.37/17.07 bar`, while A5 gives `17.07/2.37 bar`. They appear interchanged, but both printings remain retained without adopting a correction. The source's rounded state tables omit absolute stream flows, compositions, enthalpies and refrigerant totals, so they cannot close complete material/energy balances even after resolving these labels.

## Economics and annual basis

Equation 7's 20-year, 10% capital-recovery factor is independently verified by discounted cash flow. The printed tank and non-tank annualization is compatible with the displayed costs. The fixed-volume tank cost and density-related transported-mass denominator are separate effects. The tank share is about 69.5% of TAC in Case 1-2 at 0.05 mol%, so the statement that it is always more than 70% has a displayed exception.

Literal Equation 8, `OPEX = Wdot × SFOC × Costfuel`, has units USD/hour. Annual OPEX requires integration or a sum over operating hours. The available laden emissions partition bounds fuel expenditure, and nonnegative ballast purge/power gives a conditional upper bound; no ballast split or corrected source OPEX is invented. The nomenclature's ballast-time description also repeats “laden voyage.” These notation issues do not by themselves establish a numerical error in the authors' unpublished annual calculation.

All eighteen CPT entries are compatible with Equation 13 using printed TAC, exact scenario tax and transported mass when their displayed rounding intervals are propagated. These intervals are not measurement uncertainties. Reported minimum CPT of 8.90–9.29 USD/t transported remains a conditional tank/re-liquefaction index. These costs are evaluated at the reported emission-minimizing designs, not independently optimized total transport costs. The index excludes complete vessel, route, capture and injection costs and uses transported mass rather than avoided or permanently stored CO₂.

The six-voyage and 8000-hour assumptions leave 760 h in a non-leap year, without an observed loading, maintenance or weather schedule. Equation 12 labels cargo as unloaded per voyage and then subtracts purge; native gross/net cargo, density and heel conventions are needed to verify the denominator and exclude double-counting. The cited 150 USD/t carbon-price assumption is not verified applicable law. Carbon-price curves and Appendix B's approximate venting crossings remain case comparisons; the full venting case and reoptimization are unreproduced.

## Physical datum and pressure observations

Table 1 uses depths below mean sea level (MSL). Ground elevations are 205.7 m above MSL at CCS1 and 203.9 m at VW1. Adding the appropriate local elevation converts depth below MSL to depth below ground; it does not establish a Kelly-bushing datum. The CCS1 pressure gauge is at 1717.5 m below MSL, or 1923.2 m below ground. Perforations at 1917.8–1926.9 and 1930.9–1938.5 m below MSL give 16.7 m total perforated length, a 20.7 m outer span and a 4 m gap. Gauge-to-upper-perforation separation is 200.3 m. These are different supports from a model's active injection-zone thickness.

VW1 lies 305 m from CCS1 and has eleven ports. The source shifts its logs downward by 13.1 m to impose a common layered model. That modelling alignment is separate from the physical datum conversion. Applying the same shift to pressure-port extraction is only a conditional candidate until the native monitor mapping is obtained.

A [related-source comparison](../outputs/comprehensive_review/B1-18_depth_comparison.csv) converts B1-18's zones 1–6 to below-ground depths and compares B1-16 Figure 4's feet labels. Residual differences are 4.63–4.84 m across those six ports. This nearly common offset is a useful datum-provenance lead, but no undocumented correction is adopted. B1-16's Zone 4 prose value of 2128 m still conflicts with its 6838 ft figure label. This comparison is not a direct citation-support edge and does not verify either paper's raw pressure calibration.

The reported practical pressure resolution of about 1000 Pa is not an accuracy bound, independent noise standard deviation or sensor covariance. Data are hourly averages; manual outlier removal, outages, sampling periods and early Zone 3/4 drift corrections affect coverage. Zone 1 is excluded from the fit as unreliable even though it appears in Figure 4. Early Zone 5 downtime changes the gradient interval to Zones 4–6. Exact baselines, filtering and drift algorithms remain missing.

Figure 1 expressly leaves injector pressure uncorrected for the brine/CO₂ column between the gauge and perforations. Subtracting initial pressure removes a constant offset, not a changing column head or flow loss. Upper-zone nondetection has a monitored time, location and resolution scope; small late shallow-pressure variations visible in Figure 1 are not converted into leakage or a zero-leakage bound.

## Diagnostic equations and their scope

The buildup diagnostic divides each zone's pressure change by Zone 2's pressure change. A zero denominator is undefined and a small or falling denominator can produce large ratios; the paper acknowledges rate-change and shut-in spikes. Our synthetic example illustrates this algebra without digitising an unverified native time series.

The vertical-gradient diagnostic divides the current lower-minus-upper pressure difference by its initial value for the same depth interval. With depth positive downward, the brine Darcy relation is `qz = −(k/μ)(dp/dz − ρg)`. A normalized gradient above unity suggests upward brine flow only under the matching density, mobility, sign and initial-hydrostatic assumptions. It is not a universal multiphase plume-height measurement. Conditional four-pressure error examples illustrate sensitivity near unity; the assumed ±1000 Pa error bound is explicitly an analyst example, not an interpretation of the reported resolution as an actual error distribution.

Figure 2 normalizes logs using different limits in the two wells: gamma ray 26–227 API versus 12–188 API, and literal porosity limits 0.01–26.6% versus 0.01–37%. The independent min–max function preserves percent/fraction equivalence and does not invent clipping. Missing raw logs, facies choices and SDR inversion prevent reproduction of the actual layered property field. Two aligned logs do not identify a unique laterally continuous geology.

## Constitutive, viscosity and calibration qualifications

The model is radially symmetric, perfectly layered and isothermal at 49°C. Salinity is neglected despite reported total dissolved solids above 200000 ppm. The paper literally prints pure-water and brine viscosities of `0.56 × 10⁻⁴` and `0.8 × 10⁻⁴ Pa s`. Their ratio is 0.7, but the absolute values remain on HOLD. An independent pinned CoolProp 8.0.0 pure-water reference at 49°C and 18/20/24 MPa gives approximately 0.0005594–0.0005607 Pa s, roughly ten times the printed pure-water value. The [reference output](../outputs/comprehensive_review/B1-18_water_unit_reference.csv) is a unit-consistency check, not an actual-brine calculation, exponent correction or replacement author input. Kestin (1981), original code units and the reported approximately 150 mD permeability basis remain unverified.

Scaling permeability by a viscosity ratio can preserve a selected mobility; it does not preserve density, solubility, capillarity or the full saline flow problem. Pore compressibility `10⁻¹⁰ Pa⁻¹` is fitted. Relative permeability uses a Corey liquid residual saturation of 0.65 and gas residual of zero; the capillary parameterization has liquid residual 0.60, maximum liquid saturation 0.999, λ = 0.5 and `Pmax = 2.4 × 10⁷ Pa`. This 24 MPa parameter is a capillary-pressure quantity, not a BHP or fracture limit. A relative-permeability residual is not a hard maximum on every simulated gas saturation.

Equation 1 gives `Pc(Sl) = sqrt[(kref/φref)/(k/φ)] Pc_ref(Sl)`. Its dimensionless relative scale is reconstructed with positive-domain guards. Absolute Berea reference properties/curve and exact native constitutive definitions are missing. The source uses horizontal permeability below 1915 m and vertical permeability above it; no complete capillary model is claimed reproduced.

The first 40 days of pressures are used for fitting, followed by comparison over two years, with increasing mismatch acknowledged. Individual horizontal permeabilities and the liquid residual saturation are subsequently adjusted to match later CO₂ arrival while preserving average injection-interval permeability. March 2012 arrival at Zone 3 and July 2012 arrival at Zone 2 therefore participate in the final calibration. The final arrival agreement is not an independent holdout. The authors explicitly describe the history match as nonunique and defer rigorous inverse/sensitivity work.

## Reconstructed skin and geometry components

Equation 2 is `s = (k/ks − 1) ln(rs/rw)`. With printed `s = −0.85807`, `rw = 0.12 m` and `rs = 0.29 m`, `ln(rs/rw) = 0.88238918` and `ks/k = 36.28367`. Finite positive permeability requires `s > −ln(rs/rw)`; the conditional critical outer radius is about 0.28303 m. This close geometric bound explains strong local sensitivity. Independent integration of radial resistance checks the algebra. A large local annular permeability ratio is not the same factor in whole-well flow or annual reservoir service; separate fixed-state synthetic radial examples demonstrate that distinction.

Appendix A's radial counts and widths reconstruct 67 intervals to a radius of 100000 m. Its bottom-up 89 one-metre and 93 five-metre layers reconstruct 182 vertical intervals, 554 m thickness, bottom depth 1974 m, top depth 1420 m and a resolution transition at 1885 m below MSL. The [independent diagram](../outputs/comprehensive_review/B1-18_geometry.png) and [12194-cell geometry](../outputs/comprehensive_review/B1-18_geometry.csv.gz) retain these exact extents. Per-radian bulk volume is `(ro² − ri²) dz / 2`; full-circle volume multiplies by `2π`. Neither is a pore-volume resource or injection-service estimate.

The source specifies twenty well cells with porosity 0.98, permeability `10⁻⁶ m²` and no capillary pressure, removed connections above/below the well, four disconnected gap cells, shifted nodes near interfaces and injection into the top well cell. Exact indices, connection files and pressure extraction are unavailable. Reconstructing geometric intervals does not produce a runnable native TOUGH MESH, constitutive field or simulation case.
